%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{The Default Answer: Bigger}
\begin{itemize}
\item Usual path: pick the biggest model, call it over the internet (an API), pay per token (a token is a word or part of a word).
\item It works, until the bill, the delay, the connection or the data rules get in the way.
\item Frugal AI asks: what is the smallest thing that does this job?
\item Not a worse AI, a fitted one: right size, right place, right task.
\item This talk: why, how small models work, where they run, two projects, and the honest limits.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{What One Prompt Costs}
\begin{center}
\adjustbox{max width=0.8\linewidth, max totalheight=0.40\textheight}{\includegraphics{frugal_energy_per_prompt}}
\end{center}
\begin{itemize}
\item Unit: 1 Wh (watt-hour) is the energy a 1 W device uses in one hour. A 10 W LED bulb uses 10 Wh per hour.
\item \textbf{Google, measured}: a typical Gemini text prompt uses 0.24 Wh, like that bulb for about a minute and a half.
	\begin{itemize}
	\item It counts the whole service: servers, idle spare machines and data centre overhead.
	\end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Benchmark, estimated}: a long prompt on the hungriest of 30 commercial models may use over 29 Wh, like that bulb for about three hours.
	\begin{itemize}
	\item It is worked out from public data, not measured.
	\end{itemize}
\item Different methods, different prompts: read the range, not a ranking.
\end{itemize}
{\tiny (Ref: Elsworth et al., arXiv 2508.15734; Jegham et al., arXiv 2505.09598)}
\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Data Centres in Context}
\begin{itemize}
\item The IEA (International Energy Agency) reports that data centres used about 415 TWh in 2024, around 1.5\% of world electricity.
\item TWh (terawatt-hour) is a billion kWh, the units on a household bill.
\item It projects about 945 TWh by 2030, more than double.
\item These are figures for the whole sector, not for any one model or prompt.
\item The lesson is not ``AI is bad'': every token has a cost, and the cheapest token is the one you did not need.
\end{itemize}
{\tiny (Ref: IEA, Energy and AI, 2025, executive summary)}
\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{What Is Frugal AI?}
\begin{itemize}
\item The Frugal AI Hub defines it as ``maximizing environmental and societal impact with minimal resources''.
\item Its example: a small language model on a Raspberry Pi (a credit-card-sized computer), giving offline healthcare in rural clinics.
\item Its July 2026 report calls India's strength ``AI that works cheaply, offline and in-language at population scale''.
\item Four words to remember: efficient, affordable, sustainable, accessible.
\end{itemize}
{\tiny (Ref: Frugal AI Hub glossary; Frugal AI Hub, Minds, Models \& Markets, July 2026)}
\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Three Levers}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Smaller model}: pick the size the job needs, then shrink it (quantisation, distillation).
\item \textbf{Closer hardware}: use the laptop, board or phone you own, offline if possible.
\item \textbf{Fewer tokens}: short prompts, a step limit, no re-sending of the whole chat.
\item One study: multi-purpose models cost orders of magnitude more per 1,000 predictions than task-specific ones, on its tasks.
	\begin{itemize}
	\item Its result, not a law for every job.
	\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
{\tiny (Ref: Luccioni, Jernite, Strubell, Power Hungry Processing, FAccT 2024)}
\end{frame}
