\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Why Authors Are Wary of AI}
\begin{itemize}
\item Your name goes on the cover. Your voice and your judgment are the product.
\item A tool that writes fluent text in seconds feels like a threat to both.
\item Publishers and readers care about originality, accuracy and trust.
\item So the question is not whether AI can write your book. It is where AI can help without replacing the author.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{The Rule of This Seminar}
\begin{center}
\Large
\textbf{You write.}

\medskip
\textbf{AI assists around the writing.}
\end{center}

\medskip
\begin{itemize}
\item The text of your book, article or paper is written by you, by hand.
% [ai-for-x: rewritten 2026-11-08 | the idea and plan are the author's own; AI only tests them]
% \item AI helps with the work that surrounds the text: ideas, research, diagrams, review, layout checks, emails.
\item The idea, the plan and the thinking are yours. AI is an assistant for the work around them: research, fact and grammar checks, diagrams, review, emails.
\item The ``first draft'' advice in AI Basics applies to emails, notes and admin text, not to your manuscript.
\item Every example today uses the CRAFT framework, so you can reuse the pattern on your own tasks.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Analytical or Generative AI}
\begin{columns}
    \begin{column}[T]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{Analytical: reads and reports}
    \begin{itemize}
    \item Spelling and grammar checks
    \item Search and summaries
    \item Lists of inconsistencies
    \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}[T]{0.48\linewidth}
    \textbf{Generative: makes new content}
    \begin{itemize}
    \item Drafting paragraphs
    \item Rewriting your sentences
    \item Producing images
    \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
\end{columns}
\medskip
\begin{itemize}
\item You already use analytical AI in your word processor, your search engine and your bookshop recommendations.
\item A grammar tool that rewrites your sentence is doing generative work.
\item Platforms and publishers ask mainly about generated text and images. Rules differ, so read each one.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Where Authors Draw the Line}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Most accepted:} spelling and grammar, research, social media captions, admin.
\item \textbf{Debated:} brainstorming, plotting, outlining, character ideas.
\item \textbf{Most contested:} editing your prose, writing scenes, producing the final text.
\item Authors differ, and thoughtful authors respect that. Choose your own line before you start.
\item \textbf{This seminar's line:} AI helps around your text and never writes it.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
