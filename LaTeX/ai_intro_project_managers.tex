\section[Why]{Why This Matters Now}
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{}
\begin{center}
{\Large Why This Matters Now}

{\small AI and the PM Profession}
\end{center}
\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Why PM and Program Manager Roles Are Facing Disruption}

\begin{itemize}
  \item A large share of a PM's week is spent on tasks AI is now good at:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Drafting status reports, schedules, and meeting minutes
      \item Breaking a scope statement into tasks (Work Breakdown Structure)
      \item Scanning updates across a dozen threads to spot a risk before it becomes an issue
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Generative and Agentic AI} (AI that plans and carries out multi-step tasks on its own, not just one-shot answers) is strongest exactly here:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Reading and structuring large amounts of text
      \item Summarizing and spotting patterns
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The uncomfortable question:} if AI can draft the plan, spot the risk, and summarize the meeting, what is left for the PM to do?
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Myths vs.\ Reality, What Every PM Must Know}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Myth:} AI will replace the Project Manager.\\
    \textbf{Reality:} AI replaces \textit{mechanical} work (drafting, formatting, first-pass analysis), not judgment, negotiation, or accountability.

  \item \textbf{Myth:} AI plans are ready to execute as-is.\\
    \textbf{Reality:}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item AI drafts a plausible plan from limited context
      \item It doesn't know your vendor's reliability, sponsor's politics, or team's velocity
      \item PM judgment turns the draft into a real plan
    \end{itemize}

  \item \textbf{Myth:} Using AI is a shortcut that signals you're not doing the job properly.\\
    \textbf{Reality:}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Not \textit{less} work, \textit{higher-value} work
      \item More time on stakeholders, negotiation, unblocking
      \item Less time re-typing the same status update
    \end{itemize}

  \item \textbf{Myth:} You need to learn to code to use any of this.\\
    \textbf{Reality:} Plain-English prompts, no coding required.
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{The Hinton Lesson, A Role Is Not One Task}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Geoffrey Hinton (2016): predicted radiologists would be obsolete within five years
  \item A decade on: radiologists are still here, and in demand
  \item \textbf{Why?} Radiology is roughly 24--25 distinct tasks, not one:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Automated: reading an image for a pattern
      \item Not automated: weighing a finding against history, spotting a comorbidity, talking to the patient
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The PM parallel:} your role is a bundle of tasks too:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Automated: drafting, summarizing, first-pass scheduling
      \item Not automated: judgment, negotiation, accountability
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The real skill going forward:} learning to tell which of your tasks is which.
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{From Task Execution to Strategic Orchestration}

\textbf{The PM's new value proposition:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Old value:} build the schedule, track the risks, write the status report
  \item \textbf{New value:} direct AI to draft schedule, risk register, and status report in minutes
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Then apply the judgment, context, and stakeholder relationships that make it \textit{actually usable}
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Analogy:} less clerk filling in templates, more editor-in-chief
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Sets direction, reviews drafts critically
      \item Decides what goes out the door
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.3em}
\textbf{Plain English:} AI is a fast, patient junior coordinator, it still needs a PM to set the goal and catch its mistakes.

\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Quick Check: Why This Matters Now}
\begin{block}{Think About It}
Radiology survived AI disruption because it's actually $\sim$24 distinct tasks, not one. Applying that same logic, which is the most defensible reason a PM role won't be fully automated even as AI gets better at each individual task?
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[A.] PM work is legally required to have a human in the role.
  \item[B.] AI is too slow to keep up with a PM's daily workload.
  \item[C.] A PM's job is a bundle of tasks: only some (drafting, summarizing, first-pass scheduling) are automatable, others (judgment, negotiation, accountability) are not, and automating the first set doesn't collapse the second.
  \item[D.] Companies prefer to pay humans over AI regardless of capability.
\end{enumerate}
\end{block}
\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Quick Check: Why This Matters Now}
\begin{block}{Answer}
\textbf{Correct: C.} A role is a bundle of tasks, not a single skill. Automating the mechanical ones (drafting, summarizing, scheduling) doesn't touch the ones needing judgment, negotiation, and accountability. (A) and (D) dodge the actual logic; (B) will stop being true as AI speeds up.
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\section[Plan]{AI-Powered Project Planning}
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{}
\begin{center}
{\Large AI-Powered Project Planning}
\end{center}
\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{What Is AI-Powered Project Planning?}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Traditional:} PM manually builds a Work Breakdown Structure (WBS), estimates, and sequences tasks, often from a blank template
  \item \textbf{AI-assisted:} Describe the goal, scope, and constraints in plain English.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item AI proposes a first-draft WBS and sequencing in seconds
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What doesn't change:} PM still validates every task against real capacity, dependencies, and constraints AI cannot see
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{From Manual WBS to AI-Assisted WBS}

\begin{center}
\adjustbox{max width=\linewidth}{%
\begin{tabular}{|p{0.15\linewidth}|p{0.40\linewidth}|p{0.40\linewidth}|}
\hline
\textbf{Step} & \textbf{Manual (today)} & \textbf{AI-Assisted (now available)} \\
\hline
First draft & 1--2 hours from a blank template & Under 5 minutes from a project brief \\
Task granularity & Depends on PM's memory of past projects & Consistent, pulls patterns from thousands of similar plans \\
Estimation & PM's gut feel or historical spreadsheet & AI proposes a starting estimate; PM adjusts using real team data \\
Missed tasks & Common, especially in unfamiliar domains & AI flags standard tasks a PM may not have worked with before \\
Ownership of the plan & PM & Still PM: AI drafts, PM decides \\
\hline
\end{tabular}%
}
\end{center}

\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Hands-On Exercise: Generating a Work Breakdown Structure}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Activity:} Turn a one-line project idea into a structured WBS.
  \item \textbf{Try this prompt} (with any general-purpose AI assistant):\\
    \textit{``You are an experienced Project Manager. I am launching [your project, one sentence]. Break this into a 3-level Work Breakdown Structure: Phases, Deliverables, and Tasks. For each task, suggest a rough duration and the skill needed.''}
  \item \textbf{Discuss:} What did it get right? What did it miss that only you would know?
  \item \textbf{Key insight:} AI gives you a strong first draft in minutes. Your job shifts from \textit{creating} the WBS to \textit{critiquing and correcting} it.
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Prompting Patterns for Project Planning}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Role Prompting:} ``You are a senior Program Manager in [your industry]. Review this schedule for unrealistic durations.''\\
    \textit{Why it works: Activates domain-appropriate scrutiny, not generic advice.}

  \item \textbf{Context + Constraints:} Always give AI: the project goal, team size, hard deadlines, and any constraints (budget, vendor dependency, regulatory approval).\\
    \textit{``Given a 6-person team and a fixed launch date of [date], which of these 20 tasks are at risk of slipping?''}

  \item \textbf{Structured Output:} Ask for a specific format (table, Gantt-ready CSV, RAID log columns, Risks/Assumptions/Issues/Dependencies) so it plugs directly into your tools.

  \item \textbf{Iterate:} Treat the first AI output as a draft. ``Now re-sequence assuming Task 4 depends on an external vendor.''
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Quick Check: AI-Powered Project Planning}
\begin{block}{Think About It}
AI hands you a WBS in under five minutes that looks complete and well-organized. What is the one question you must still ask before you trust it?
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[A.] Does it use the exact WBS template my company prefers?
  \item[B.] Does it reflect the real team capacity, dependencies, and constraints AI never saw?
  \item[C.] Is it formatted as a table instead of a numbered list?
  \item[D.] Did it take less than five minutes to generate?
\end{enumerate}
\end{block}
\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Quick Check: AI-Powered Project Planning}
\begin{block}{Answer}
\textbf{Correct: B.} A polished draft is not a validated plan, only the PM knows the real capacity, dependencies, and constraints. (A), (C), (D) are cosmetic or speed properties, not validation.
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\section[Decision]{AI Decision Support}
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{}
\begin{center}
{\Large AI Decision Support}
\end{center}
\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{What Is AI Decision Support?}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Not AI \textit{making} the decision, AI \textit{laying out the trade-offs}
  \item So the PM decides faster, with more complete information
  \item Common PM decisions AI can help structure:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Slip the deadline, add resources, or cut scope, which trade-off best serves the goal?
      \item Which of three vendors best fits this project's risk profile?
      \item Given current velocity, is the committed date still realistic?
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The PM's role sharpens, not shrinks:} which trade-off matters most is still a human judgment call
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Decision Support Use Cases}

\begin{center}
\adjustbox{max width=\linewidth}{%
\begin{tabular}{|p{0.35\linewidth}|p{0.60\linewidth}|}
\hline
\textbf{Decision} & \textbf{How AI Supports It} \\
\hline
Schedule vs.\ scope trade-off & Lists what each option costs/saves, in time, budget, and risk \\
Resource reallocation & Simulates ``what if we move 2 people from Task A to Task B'' \\
Vendor/tool selection & Summarizes trade-offs across 3--4 options against your stated criteria \\
Go/no-go on a milestone & Synthesizes status data into a single readiness score with reasoning shown \\
\hline
\end{tabular}%
}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.3em}
\textbf{Critical rule:} Always ask AI to show \textit{why}, not just \textit{what}. A recommendation without visible reasoning is just a confident guess.

\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Hands-On Exercise: AI Decision-Support Prompt}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Activity:} Use AI to lay out a real trade-off you're facing (or a sample one).
  \item \textbf{Try this prompt:}\\
    \textit{``I am 3 weeks behind on a 12-week project. I can: (a) add 2 contractors for the last 4 weeks, (b) cut a non-critical feature, or (c) push the launch date by 2 weeks. For each option, list the cost, the risk, and who I'd need to convince.''}
  \item \textbf{Discuss:} Did AI surface an option or risk you hadn't considered? Where would you push back on its reasoning?
  \item \textbf{Key insight:} This is not about AI choosing for you, it's about walking into the sponsor conversation with all three options already priced out.
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Quick Check: AI Decision Support}
\begin{block}{Think About It}
AI lays out three options for your schedule slip, each with a clear cost and risk. Why is this still not the same as AI making the decision for you?
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[A.] AI cannot format the options into a table.
  \item[B.] Choosing which trade-off matters most to this sponsor and team is a judgment call AI cannot make.
  \item[C.] AI is only allowed to suggest two options at a time.
  \item[D.] The options AI lists are always factually wrong.
\end{enumerate}
\end{block}
\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Quick Check: AI Decision Support}
\begin{block}{Answer}
\textbf{Correct: B.} AI can price out the options, but choosing which trade-off matters most depends on relationships and context it doesn't have. Structuring a decision and making it are different jobs; (A), (C), (D) aren't real limitations.
\end{block}
\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Live Demo Setup}

\textbf{Sample project brief} (used for the rest of this demo):

\vspace{0.3em}
\textit{``Launch a mobile app for internal expense reporting at a 200-person company. Timeline: 10 weeks. Team: 1 PM, 2 developers, 1 designer, 1 QA. Must integrate with the existing HR system. Depends on an external vendor for the payment-gateway module.''}

\vspace{0.5em}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Follow along on your own device with any general-purpose AI assistant.
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Step 1: AI Generates a Work Breakdown Structure}

\textbf{Prompt used:}\\
\textit{``Using the project brief above, generate a 3-level WBS: Phases, Deliverables, Tasks. Flag any task that depends on the external payment-gateway vendor.''}

\vspace{0.3em}
\textbf{What to watch for in the live output:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Does it correctly isolate the vendor-dependent tasks?
  \item Are the phase names sensible for a 10-week timeline, or overly generic?
  \item What would \textit{you} add that AI, lacking context on this specific company's HR system, couldn't know?
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Step 2: AI Generates a Risk \& Dependency Register}

\textbf{Prompt used:}\\
\textit{``From the same brief, generate a RAID log. Pay special attention to the HR system integration and the external payment-gateway vendor as dependency sources.''}

\vspace{0.3em}
\textbf{What to watch for in the live output:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Did it flag the vendor dependency as the highest-impact risk (it should)?
  \item Did it surface any risk category you hadn't listed yourself (e.g. data-access approval for the HR system)?
  \item Notice: the log is a strong \textit{starting point} for the room's discussion, not a finished risk assessment.
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Step 3: AI Decision Support, ``Should We Compress the Schedule?''}

\textbf{Prompt used:}\\
\textit{``The vendor says the payment-gateway module will be 2 weeks late. I can: (a) launch without payment integration and add it later, (b) delay the whole launch by 2 weeks, or (c) run QA in parallel with the vendor's remaining work. Lay out the trade-offs for each.''}

\vspace{0.3em}
\textbf{What to watch for in the live output:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Are the trade-offs specific to \textit{this} project, or generic boilerplate?
  \item Which option would you actually recommend to the sponsor, and why might your answer differ from AI's framing?
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Debrief: What This Demo Shows, and Its Limits}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it shows:} first-draft WBS, RAID log, and decision analysis in under 10 minutes, work that used to take an hour or more
  \item \textbf{Its limits:} generic to the \textit{stated} brief, knows nothing about your real vendor history, team morale, or sponsor risk appetite
  \item \textbf{The real workflow:} AI drafts in minutes, you refine with your context in minutes more, still far faster than a blank page
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Quick Check: Live Demo}

The live demo produced a WBS, a RAID log, and a decision analysis in under 10 minutes, all generic to the brief as stated. What would change every one of those outputs?
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[A.] Running the exact same prompt a second time.
  \item[B.] Real information only you have: vendor track record, team morale, sponsor risk appetite.
  \item[C.] Asking AI to use a different output font.
  \item[D.] Nothing, the outputs stay identical regardless of context.
\end{enumerate}
\end{block}
\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Quick Check: Live Demo}
\begin{block}{Answer}
\textbf{Correct: B.} Details AI can't see, vendor track record, team morale, sponsor risk appetite, politics, would change every output. The demo compresses drafting, not judgment; (A), (C), (D) change nothing that matters.
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\section[Tools]{Practical Tools \& Workflows}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Risks and Guardrails Before You Start}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Confidentiality:} Don't paste unreleased financials, vendor terms, or personal team data into a public AI tool without org approval.
  \item \textbf{Hallucination:} AI can state a risk, cost, or dependency that sounds precise but is invented.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Verify any number before it goes into a real plan.
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Over-reliance:} A confident-sounding draft is still a draft.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Treat every AI output as a first pass, not a final answer.
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Check policy first:} Many organizations already restrict which AI tools may touch project data.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Ask before you paste.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

\begin{center}
\adjustbox{max width=\linewidth}{%
