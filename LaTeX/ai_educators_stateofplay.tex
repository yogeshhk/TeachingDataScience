\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Quick Check: A Guess Before the Numbers}
\begin{itemize}
\item What fraction of US teachers used an AI tool during the 2024--25 school year?
\begin{itemize}
\item A) About 15\%
\item B) About 35\%
\item C) About 60\%
\item D) About 85\%
\end{itemize}
\item Take a moment, pick one before the next slide.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{frame}[fragile]\frametitle{Quick Check: Answer}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Both C and D are defensible}, depending on which survey you read.
\item A Gallup--Walton Family Foundation poll of US public school teachers found \textbf{60\%} used AI in the 2024--25 school year.
\item A separate CDT (Center for Democracy \& Technology) survey put it at \textbf{85\%}.
\item Either way: teacher AI adoption roughly doubled year over year, from about \textbf{32\% in 2024 to 61\% in 2025} on aggregate tracking.
\end{itemize}
{\tiny (Refs: Gallup--Walton Family Foundation Voice of Educator survey; CDT ``Educator Perspectives on AI in Schools''.)}
\end{frame}

\begin{itemize}

\end{itemize}

\medskip
\textbf{Notice what all four top uses have in common:} none of them is ``teach the class'' or ``know the student.''
